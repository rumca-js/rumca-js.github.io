Title: YouTube Is Not What You Think It Is

Stop.

Just stop for a second.

Before you watch another video… ask yourself:

Why are you here?

Are you trying to learn something?

Or are you just scrolling because it’s easy?

Because most of the time—it’s not a decision.

What we sohuld care about is author is intent.

Some videos are designed to teach you something.

Others are designed to keep you watching.

And those are not the same thing.

The uncomfortable truth is this:

YouTube does not reward value.

It rewards watch time.

The algorithm doesn’t ask:

“Did this help you?”

It asks:

“Did you stay?”

And that changes everything.

Because the best content is not always the most visible.

And the most visible content is often not the best.

So what can you do?

Simple.

Be selective.

Assume content is low-value—until it proves otherwise.

Don’t just ask:

“Is this interesting?”

Ask:

“Is this worth my time?”

Let’s start with the good side.

There are channels that genuinely respect your time.

They explain complex ideas clearly. They give you knowledge you didn’t have before. They leave you better than you were.

Good science and engineering channels are a great example.

They’re rare—because they’re hard to make.

Explaining something complicated in a simple way takes real effort.

But when it’s done right, it stands out immediately.

The same goes for educational content.

Music theory, history, geography—there’s a huge amount of valuable information out there if you look for it.

Even tech channels can be useful—when they go beyond headlines and actually explain what’s happening.

And some content helps you make decisions.

Movie reviews, for example, can save you time instead of wasting it.

Even corporate channels—surprisingly—can be useful.

They’re focused. They show products, systems, ideas.

They’re not always exciting, but they’re usually not trying to trap you either.

Then there’s the middle ground.

Content that depends on how you use it.

Sports, for example.

If you care about it, it has value.

If you don’t, it’s just noise.

Same with regional content—entire ecosystems exist in different languages and countries.

You’re only seeing a tiny fraction of what YouTube actually is.

And then there are “top 10” or list-style videos.

Easy to watch. Sometimes useful.

But usually shallow.

They’re designed for continuous consumption.

Not deep understanding.

And finally—the part most people spend their time in.

Content that is optimized for attention.

Not value.

Attention.

This includes a huge portion of entertainment content.

Videos that don’t teach you anything new—but keep you watching anyway.

React content is a perfect example.

You’re not even watching the original idea anymore.

You’re watching someone else react to it.

It feels like shared experience—but it’s mostly passive.

Then there are outrage-driven channels.

Everything is framed as conflict.

Everything is emotional.

Because emotion keeps you watching.

Not understanding—watching.

And some content goes even further.

Fear-based videos.

Low-quality children’s content.

Things designed to overstimulate, not inform.

So stop.

Think about it. Think about your life.

It is hard to use YouTube in a correct way, as it can push you toward brainrot.

Currently I recommend using RSS channels as input to have super control over your feed.

YouTube can be one of the best learning tools ever created.

Or one of the most efficient ways to waste your life.

And the difference…

is entirely up to you.
